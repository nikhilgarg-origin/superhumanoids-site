Super·Humanoids

We will do what humanoids cannot.

The human form is a starting point, not a ceiling. We are building machines that begin where the humanoid ends — reconfiguring their own bodies to suit the task, instead of forcing every task through one fixed shape.

Why the name

A humanoid can only ever be as capable as the body it copies.

Two arms, two legs, one configuration. It is a remarkable design — and a hard limit. Every humanoid robot inherits the constraints of the thing it was modelled on, including the ones evolution never had a reason to solve.

We are not trying to build a better copy of a person. We are building what comes after one. That is why we are called Super Humanoids.

The ambition

Transformers were never a cartoon. They were a specification.

A machine that changes its own configuration — folding, extending, redistributing its mass to walk, climb, carry or fit — is the thing a generation of us grew up watching and assumed was impossible. It is not impossible. It is a control problem, a materials problem, and above all a learning problem, and the path runs through foundation models rather than bespoke mechanism design.

Nobody has built one. We intend to make them real.

Approach

01

One policy, many bodies

A single model family spanning perception, whole-body control and manipulation — trained across tasks and across morphologies rather than engineered for one.

02

Simulation at scale

Policies learn across massively parallel physics simulation, where a machine can attempt a task more times in an hour than a human could in a lifetime.

03

Closing the reality gap

A policy is only as good as its transfer to the physical world. Our development loop is built around narrowing that gap, not around benchmark scores.
