Today, robot intelligence is trapped inside the body it was trained on. Change the legs, the sensors or the reach, and most of what the robot knew has to be learned again.

We think that is a data problem, not a law of nature. A model that has seen many bodies should learn what they share: contact, balance, reach, cause and effect.

Pretrain once.
Adapt to each body.

  1. 1

    Describe the body

    Joints, limits, sensors and mass go in as input. The model knows what it is driving before it moves.

  2. 2

    Learn from many machines

    Pretrain on simulated experience across many bodies, tasks, contacts and failures, so physical knowledge is shared instead of siloed.

  3. 3

    Adapt, then ground it

    Fine-tune for a new body with little data, then close the gap between simulation and hardware with measured real-world runs.

Proposed milestones

From a multi-body dataset to a model that transfers to a body it has never seen.

  2. Multi-body dataset

    Simulated experience across simplified bodies, tasks, contacts and failures.

  3. Shared pretraining

    One model family trained across several bodies, replacing one policy per body.

  4. Transfer benchmark

    Adaptation speed and task success measured on a held-out body.

  5. Hardware validation

    A narrow capability moved to a physical rig, with the reality gap measured.

Research stage, before a first model

We are not showing you a trained model yet.

The first proof is narrow on purpose: one shared policy that completes a measurable task across several simulated bodies. Every robot on this page is a simulation render of an open model, not hardware we have built.

The body is only the beginning.

We are looking for industrial partners with hard, multi-body problems and engineers who want to test this idea with us.
